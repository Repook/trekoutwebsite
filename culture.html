Māori Language

Te Reo Māori is one of New Zealand’s official languages and is an important part of the country’s cultural identity. Although English is the most commonly spoken language, Te Reo Māori can be heard in schools, media, government, and everyday conversation. Many New Zealanders use common Māori words and phrases in daily life.

Haka and Greetings

The haka is a traditional Māori performance involving powerful movements, chanting, and facial expressions. It is often performed to welcome guests, celebrate achievements, or show unity and strength. Greetings are also an important part of New Zealand culture, with phrases such as “kia ora” commonly used to say hello, thank you, or express appreciation.

New Zealand Slang

New Zealand English includes a wide range of unique slang words and expressions. Locals are often called “Kiwis,” while phrases like “sweet as” mean “great” or “no problem.” Slang reflects the relaxed and friendly nature of New Zealand culture and adds personality to everyday conversations.
