History

Welcome to TREKOUT — your guide to building a life in New Zealand

Early Māori Settlement

The history of New Zealand begins with the arrival of Māori voyagers from Polynesia over 700 years ago. Using advanced navigation skills, they travelled across the Pacific Ocean and settled throughout the islands, developing rich traditions, unique tribal communities, and a deep connection to the land and sea. Māori culture remains an important part of New Zealand’s identity today.

European Arrival & Colonisation

European explorers first reached New Zealand in the 17th century, with Dutch navigator Abel Tasman arriving in 1642, followed later by British explorer James Cook in 1769. During the 1800s, increasing European settlement led to major social and political change. The signing of the Treaty of Waitangi in 1840 between Māori chiefs and the British Crown became one of the most significant events in New Zealand history.

Modern New Zealand

Over time, New Zealand developed into an independent and multicultural nation known for its natural beauty, innovation, and strong sense of community. Today, the country celebrates both Māori and European heritage while continuing to grow as a diverse society shaped by people from around the world. New Zealand is recognised internationally for its unique culture, environmental values, and progressive outlook.
